# Bumble Settings, Badges & Messaging Guide

Optimizing badges, filters, and messaging behaviour for visibility and quality matches. Evidence tiers are noted where a claim is more than product description.

---

## How Bumble Works Now (verify — the product changed in 2026)

| Mechanic | Current state | Source tier |
|----------|---------------|-------------|
| Who messages first | Anyone in a match, regardless of gender (since August 2026) | Bumble announcement, reported by TechCrunch and NBC News |
| Match window | 72 hours to send the first message; the other person then gets 72 hours to reply. No message either way and the match expires | Bumble support / press |
| Opening Moves | Retired in August 2026 | Bumble support (updated 10 August 2026) |
| Low-effort openers | Bumble flags "hi" / "hey" and nudges a more personal message, but doesn't block it | Bumble support |
| Extend | Extends an expiring match; free users get limited extends, Boost and up get unlimited | Bumble support |
| Rematch | Reconnect with an expired match | Paid (Boost and up) |
| Swiping | Being phased out in select markets during 2026 in favour of "Bee" (AI matchmaker) and chapter-style profiles | Bumble announcement; ask the user what their app shows |

**Why the messaging change matters for the profile:** under the old rule, a man's profile only had to survive a swipe; a woman's had to survive a swipe *and* give her something to write. Now both profiles have to do both jobs. Every line of copy should be checked against one question: could a stranger send a decent first message from this in under a minute?

## Distance Settings

### The Tradeoff
- **Too narrow:** Missing good people just outside radius
- **Too wide:** Matches from impractical distances, dilutes pool

### Recommendations by Context

| Situation | Suggested Radius |
|-----------|------------------|
| Major city (London, NYC) | 15-25 miles / 25-40 km |
| Suburban area | 25-50 miles / 40-80 km |
| Rural area | 50+ miles / 80+ km |
| Open to relocating | Maximum, or use Travel Mode (Premium) |

### Reality Check Questions
- "How far would you actually travel for a first date?"
- "Would you date someone who lives [X] away long-term?"
- "Are you getting enough people in your current radius?"

## Age Range Settings

### Common Mistakes
- Setting too narrow based on arbitrary numbers
- Not considering who actually fits their criteria
- Mismatch between stated preference and who they're attracted to

### Reality Check
- "What age range are you genuinely open to?"
- "Have your best past relationships/dates fit this range?"
- "Is this based on real preference or just what sounds right?"

### Market Reality (Heterosexual Dating)
- Men typically set wider ranges (often younger)
- Women in 40s competing with women in 30s for men in their age range
- Very narrow ranges dramatically shrink the pool
- Consider: does the person they're looking for actually date people their age?

**The research context:** Bruch & Newman (2018, *Science Advances*) analyzed ~200,000 users and found that most people pursue partners ~25% more desirable than themselves, desirability follows a power-law distribution, and women's desirability peaked earlier than men's in their dataset. This isn't about anyone's worth — it's about understanding the market you're operating in so settings don't accidentally filter out good matches.

## Badges: The Part People Forget Is a Filter

On Bumble, badges are not decoration. Other people can filter on many of them, and they sit on the card next to the first photo. An empty badge is a hard "no" from anyone filtering on it.

### Dating intentions ("Looking for")

Up to two show on the profile. Options include a long-term relationship, life partner, marriage, fun casual dates, intimacy without commitment, ethical non-monogamy, and figuring out my dating goals (exact wording varies by version).

- **Set it.** Unset reads as evasive, and people filter on it.
- **Set it honestly.** "Wrong kind of matches" is usually an intention mismatch, not a photo problem.
- **Two is fine if both are true.** "Long-term" plus "figuring it out" is honest for a lot of people. "Life partner" plus "intimacy without commitment" is a contradiction that reads as one.

### My basics

Height, work, education, location, hometown, exercise, drinking, smoking, cannabis, kids (have / want), star sign, politics, religion, and gender identity.

**Fill the filterable ones:** height, exercise, drinking, smoking, kids, education. Leaving them blank removes the profile from anyone filtering on them (Premium users have Advanced Filters across most of these).

**Fill work and education with the interesting version.** "Makes exhibition catalogues" beats "Graphic designer". The badge is on the card; it's the first text anyone reads.

**Consider hiding if not relevant:**
- Star sign (adds nothing for most, clutters the card)
- Politics (unless it's a sort they want active)
- Religion (unless practising / important)

**Keep visible if:** it's a filter they want active, a positive differentiator, or invites conversation.

### Interests (up to 5)

Bumble offers nearly 200 interest badges across creativity, sports, going out, staying in, film & TV, reading, music, food & drink, travel, pets, values & traits, and allyship. Bumble's own numbers: 90% of members have at least two, 60% have all five (platform data, Bumble blog).

- **Use all five.** People can filter by interest, and a shared badge is the lowest-effort opener on the app.
- **Specific beats broad.** "Vinyl" over "Music". "Climbing" over "Fitness". A specific badge is a costly signal (Donath, 2007); a broad one is noise.
- **Don't duplicate the bio.** If the bio already does brutalism and pub quiz, spend the badges on the things there wasn't room for.

### Photo verification

Free. Adds the blue check. Removes the "is this real" objection before it's raised. Toma & Hancock (2010) found viewers read photo evasion as deception; verification is the opposite signal. Do it.

## Modes & Visibility

| Feature | What it does | Tier |
|---------|--------------|------|
| **Snooze** | Hides the profile for a set period without losing matches | Free |
| **Incognito** | Only people you swipe right on can see you | Premium |
| **Travel Mode** | Swipe in a city you're about to visit, for 7 days | Premium |
| **Spotlight** | Puts the profile at the front of the stack for 30 minutes | Paid a-la-carte; 1/week with Boost and Premium, 2/week with Premium+ |

**Spotlight timing:** conventional wisdom says early evening on weekdays and Sunday evening, when the most people are swiping. Bumble hasn't published the data; treat as a reasonable default, not a fact.

## Premium Tiers (2026)

| Tier | Adds |
|------|------|
| **Boost** | Unlimited swipes, unlimited Backtrack, unlimited Extends, Rematch, 1 Spotlight/week, 5 SuperSwipes/week |
| **Premium** | Everything in Boost plus Beeline (see who liked you), Incognito, Travel Mode, Advanced Filters |
| **Premium+** | Everything in Premium plus Priority Likes (shown sooner), 2 Notes/week, 2 Spotlights/week, 10 SuperSwipes/week |

SuperSwipes, Spotlights, and Notes can also be bought individually.

### Strategy by feature

- **Beeline:** the highest-value paid feature for most people. Prioritise people who already liked you; those matches convert.
- **Notes (formerly Compliments):** a message on a specific photo or prompt *before* matching, 150 characters. This is Bumble's version of a Hinge comment: a costly signal that someone actually read the profile. Rationed, so spend them where you'd regret not reaching someone.
- **SuperSwipe:** tells the other person you swiped with intent. Scarcity is the point. Don't spend them on every profile.
- **Advanced Filters:** useful for real dealbreakers, dangerous when stacked. Every filter multiplies the pool down.
- **Rematch / Extend:** worth it if matches are expiring because of *timing*, not because of the profile. If nobody opens, fix the copy first.

## Algorithm Optimization

### Activity Matters
- Daily activity (10-15 mins) keeps profile visible
- Extended inactivity = deprioritised
- *Evidence tier: conventional wisdom. Bumble hasn't published specifics, but this is consistent with how recommendation systems work and is widely reported across dating coaching.*

### Engagement Quality
- Open matches, and open them with something specific. Bumble now flags "hey" as low-effort; messages referencing specific profile content got 30-40% more replies in OkCupid's data.
- Respond to matches promptly
- Extend conversations (not one-word answers)
- *Evidence tier: platform data for the specificity claim (OkCupid). The rest is conventional wisdom.*
- *Why specific openers work (research): signaling theory (Donath, 2007) — a specific message is a costly signal requiring effort, indicating genuine interest. "Hey" is a cheap signal that communicates nothing.*

### Selectivity
- Being too selective may limit who the algorithm shows you to
- Being not selective enough means matches don't convert
- *Evidence tier: conventional wisdom. The "10-20% like rate sweet spot" is widely cited in dating coaching but has no published source. The principle (some selectivity signals quality) is plausible but unverified.*

### New-profile visibility
- A new or substantially rebuilt profile is widely believed to get a short visibility bump
- *Evidence tier: conventional wisdom. Useful mainly to explain why week 1 can look better than week 3, and why that isn't a reason to panic.*

### Bee (AI matchmaker) and chapter profiles
- Where rolled out, Bumble says Bee learns interests, lifestyle, relationship goals, and communication style before recommending people. If the user has it, specificity in copy matters *more*, not less: a matchmaker can't recommend on "love to laugh".
- *Evidence tier: Bumble announcement; mechanics unpublished.*

## Implementation Notes

When adjusting settings:

1. **Make changes incrementally** - Don't change everything at once
2. **Give time to see effect** - The system needs time to recalibrate
3. **Track what changes** - Note what settings were vs what they become
4. **Revisit after 2 weeks** - Assess if changes helped or hurt
